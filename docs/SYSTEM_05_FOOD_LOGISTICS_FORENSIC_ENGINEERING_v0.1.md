## 0. Purpose and forensic standard

System 05 isolates the donor's **food logistics subsystem**: the machinery that attempts to keep food flowing through sheep, luring, forage, farms, and associated worker/resource decisions.

The donor-level forensic decomposition identifies **food logistics** as a distinct machine and characterizes it as a system for sheep/luring/forage/farm management that maintains food continuity under uncertainty. Its disposition is **PRESERVE + IMPROVE**. fileciteturn32file0L2-L2

This distinction is important. Food logistics is not simply “make villagers gather food.” It is a continuity problem: the AI must maintain a sufficiently reliable food supply while the availability, location, safety, collection rate, worker allocation, and transition cost of food sources change over time.

The purpose of this document is therefore not to invent a generic farming algorithm. It is to recover the donor's food-continuity machinery, classify its state and authority, expose implicit transitions and resource-policy interactions, and define a stronger AEGIS-compatible interface.

**Forensic rule:** static presence establishes that code/state exists. It does not establish runtime reachability, correct writer precedence, actual gatherer behavior, resource yield, economic optimality, or strategic success.

# 1. Executive finding

The food subsystem should be treated as an **economic continuity controller** rather than a collection of gathering rules.

The donor already contains knowledge for multiple food-source regimes:

- sheep;
- luring;
- forage;
- farms;
- transitions between food sources;
- associated worker allocation and infrastructure consequences.

The forensic corpus explicitly identifies food logistics as a subsystem that manages **food continuity under uncertainty**. fileciteturn32file0L2-L2

The central architectural problem is therefore not lack of food rules. It is that food availability, food demand, worker allocation, infrastructure readiness, and strategic consumption can become coupled through procedural state.

AEGIS should not replace this knowledge with a simplistic “food priority” variable.

Instead, reconstruct food logistics as:

```text
FOOD OBSERVATION
      ↓
FOOD-SOURCE CLASSIFICATION
      ↓
FOOD SUPPLY ESTIMATE
      ↓
DEMAND / CONSUMPTION REQUIREMENT
      ↓
SOURCE SELECTION
      ↓
WORKER / INFRASTRUCTURE REQUIREMENT
      ↓
EXECUTION
      ↓
OBSERVED COLLECTION / SOURCE CHANGE
      ↓
REASSESSMENT
```

**Disposition: PRESERVE donor food-source knowledge → FORMALIZE food continuity state → separate demand from supply → improve source transitions → expose infrastructure/resource requirements → qualify before changing strategic behavior.**

# 4. Food is a flow, not a stock

A food count is an instantaneous stock measurement. Food logistics is concerned with **future flow**.

A useful conceptual distinction is:

```text
FOOD_STOCK
FOOD_INCOME_RATE
FOOD_DEMAND_RATE
FOOD_SOURCE_CAPACITY
FOOD_ACCESSIBILITY
FOOD_RISK
FOOD_CONTINUITY_HORIZON
```

These are not interchangeable.

For example:

```text
Food stock = high
Food income = collapsing
Food source = nearly exhausted
```

is not a healthy food state simply because the stock variable is currently large.

Conversely:

```text
Food stock = moderate
Food income = stable
Food source = secure
```

may be economically healthy.

This flow-oriented model is an AEGIS-GENERALIZATION. Muse must recover which components the donor actually computes before representing them as donor-native semantics.

# 6. Sheep as an information-dependent resource

Sheep are unusual because food availability depends on acquisition and spatial control before they become a stable gathering source.

The relevant conceptual chain is:

```text
SHEEP INFORMATION
      ↓
SHEEP LOCATION / AVAILABILITY
      ↓
LURE / COLLECTION REQUIREMENT
      ↓
SHEEP ACCESS
      ↓
FOOD GATHERING
      ↓
DEPLETION
      ↓
SOURCE TRANSITION
```

System 04 therefore supplies information-acquisition capability, while System 05 consumes relevant food-source observations.

System 05 should not independently invent a second scouting architecture simply because sheep information is useful.

If food logistics requires new information, it should express an **information requirement** through the System 04 interface.

# 7. Luring is a coordination problem

Luring should not be modeled solely as a gather command.

A lure requires coordination between:

- food-source location;
- desired gathering location;
- scout/lure actor;
- food workers;
- timing;
- source safety;
- transition conditions.

Conceptually:

```text
LURE CANDIDATE
    ↓
SOURCE VALID
    ↓
LURE ACTOR AVAILABLE
    ↓
DESTINATION / GATHERING AREA VALID
    ↓
LURE EXECUTION
    ↓
OBSERVE SHEEP POSITION
    ↓
CONFIRM FOOD ACCESS
```

Command issuance must not be treated as successful lure completion.

The final observation boundary is the relevant completion evidence.

# 8. Forage is a fallback source, not automatically a strategic preference

Forage can provide food continuity when sheep are unavailable, unsafe, inaccessible, or depleted.

The implementation must distinguish:

```text
FORAGE_AVAILABLE
FORAGE_ACCESSIBLE
FORAGE_ASSIGNED
FORAGE_PRODUCTIVE
```

A source existing in the world does not prove that it is operationally available to the economy.

Likewise, choosing forage does not necessarily mean the AI has made a strategic choice to abandon another food source. It may simply be an operational fallback.

# 13. Worker allocation is a control problem

Food workers should be treated as an allocation state rather than a raw count.

At minimum distinguish:

```text
DESIRED_FOOD_WORKERS
ASSIGNED_FOOD_WORKERS
PRODUCTIVE_FOOD_WORKERS
TRANSITIONING_WORKERS
UNAVAILABLE_WORKERS
```

A common failure is:

```text
desired = 8
assigned = 8
```

being interpreted as:

```text
food problem solved
```

when the eight workers are walking, blocked, gathering from an exhausted source, or waiting for infrastructure.

Therefore worker assignment and observed productivity must remain distinct.

# 14. Food worker transition costs

Changing food sources is not free.

A worker moving:

```text
sheep → farm
```

may lose productive time.

Similarly:

```text
wood → food
```

may solve an immediate food deficit while creating a future infrastructure deficit.

Food logistics should therefore expose the **transition requirement** rather than pretending every reassignment is instantaneous.

This is an AEGIS-GENERALIZATION unless the donor's exact transition accounting is recovered.

# 15. Source selection should use continuity, not only distance

The nearest food source is not necessarily the best source.

A useful conceptual evaluation is:

```text
SOURCE_VALUE =
    expected food yield
  + continuity
  + accessibility
  + safety
  - transition cost
  - infrastructure cost
  - worker travel cost
  - depletion risk
```

This is an AEGIS-GENERALIZATION, not a donor formula.

The forensic task is first to identify the donor's actual source-selection predicates and only then determine whether the donor's selection logic should be preserved, improved, or replaced.

Food continuity cannot be separated completely from safety.

```text
          ↓
FOOD SOURCE RISK
          ↓

          ↓

```

Food logistics may adapt its source choice, but it should not independently invent the military response.

# 18. Food and escrow

Food logistics consumes resources while also supporting the resource flow required by other commitments.

This creates an important interaction with Shadow's escrow system.

The economic audit establishes three relevant facts:

1. escrow can protect resources for future transactions;
2. affordability is a feasibility gate;
3. resource saving is coupled to progression/work-item state. fileciteturn34file0L2-L2



Food logistics may request resources required for continuity, but it must not silently consume another commitment's protected resources.

# 20. Food continuity horizon

The most useful derived state is not merely “food low.”

The control plane should reason about:

> How long can the current food system sustain the current demand before intervention is required?

Conceptually:

```text
CONTINUITY_HORIZON
≈ usable food + expected near-term income
  ---------------------------------------
          expected consumption rate
```

This equation is an AEGIS-GENERALIZATION and must not be presented as donor-native behavior.

It is nevertheless a useful engineering target because it turns food management from reactive stock checking into forward-looking continuity management.

# 21. Source exhaustion and transition timing

Source transitions should begin before catastrophic depletion when transition latency is significant.

For example:

```text
SOURCE_REMAINING < TRANSITION_REQUIREMENT
```

may justify beginning the next source before the current source reaches zero.

The exact predicate depends on:

- travel time;
- setup time;
- construction time;
- source accessibility;
- expected depletion rate;
- worker reassignment latency.

These are policy variables until qualified.

# 30. Static versus runtime boundary

Static evidence can establish:

- sheep/luring/forage/farm rules exist;
- food state variables exist;
- source-selection predicates exist;
- worker-allocation rules exist;
- timers exist;
- farm/food dependencies exist;
- food rules are ordered or jumped.

Runtime evidence is required to establish:

- a food source is actually detected;
- workers are assigned as intended;
- workers actually gather;
- food income changes as predicted;
- luring succeeds;
- farms are actually constructed and become operational;
- source transitions occur;
- starvation is detected at the intended point;
- food policy improves downstream production;
- food continuity improves strategic outcomes.

Never promote the first category into the second.

# 34. Failure taxonomy

System 05 must distinguish at least:

### FL-01 — Source illusion

Source exists but is inaccessible or unproductive.

### FL-02 — Worker illusion

Desired worker count equals assigned count but observed food production remains inadequate.

### FL-03 — Transition lag

Source replacement begins too late to prevent a continuity collapse.

### FL-04 — Premature transition

Workers abandon a productive source too early, creating unnecessary loss.

### FL-05 — Farm funding starvation

Farm requirement exists but competing reservations prevent timely construction.

### FL-06 — Reservation conflict

Food continuity consumes or blocks resources reserved for a higher-priority commitment.

### FL-07 — Stale source state

Depleted/unsafe source remains represented as available.

### FL-08 — Strategic leakage

Food pressure silently changes strategic objective or doctrine.

### FL-09 — Oscillation

Worker/source assignment repeatedly flips between alternatives.

### FL-10 — Emergency latch

Emergency food policy remains active after the triggering condition disappears.

### FL-11 — No-source deadlock

The system detects food failure but cannot establish a replacement requirement.

### FL-12 — False completion

A worker assignment, farm command, or lure command is treated as completed without postcondition evidence.

# 35. Hysteresis requirements

Food-source switching is particularly vulnerable to oscillation.

Do not switch on every marginal score change.

Conceptual rule:

```text
SWITCH SOURCE IF
    CURRENT_SOURCE_INVALID
    OR CURRENT_SOURCE_CANNOT_MEET_REQUIREMENT
    OR EMERGENCY
    OR
    (ALTERNATIVE_VALUE - CURRENT_VALUE) >= SOURCE_SWITCH_MARGIN
```

Additional controls may include:

- minimum source dwell time;
- transition cooldown;
- minimum viable source capacity;
- minimum worker reassignment batch;
- exhaustion prediction horizon.

These are AEGIS-GENERALIZATION until directly mapped to donor behavior.

If food infrastructure competes with a higher-priority emergency commitment, the resolution should use the established preemption architecture rather than direct overwrites.

Conceptually:

```text
FOOD COMMITMENT ACTIVE
       ↓

       ↓

       ↓
FREE ONLY ATTRIBUTABLE EXCESS
       ↓
FUND EMERGENCY
       ↓

       ↓
```

Do not release all food reservations merely because the economy entered an emergency state.

Minimum viable funding and ownership must be preserved where policy requires it.

# 38. Observability requirements

The implementation must make it possible to diagnose:

```text
Why are we short on food?
```

The answer should be classifiable as one or more of:

- insufficient food source;
- insufficient workers;
- worker travel/transition loss;
- unsafe source;
- source depletion;
- farm infrastructure unavailable;
- farm funding blocked;
- resource reservation conflict;
- strategic demand spike;
- execution failure;
- stale/incorrect observation;
- unresolved.

A single `food-low` flag is inadequate for forensic engineering.

# 40. Hard invariants

**FO-01 — Food stock is not food continuity.**

Current stock does not prove future supply adequacy.

**FO-02 — Food source existence is not operational availability.**

A source must be accessible and productive for the economy to rely on it.

**FO-03 — Worker assignment is not productivity.**

Assigned workers must not be treated as successful gathering without appropriate observation.

**FO-04 — Source selection is not strategic doctrine.**

Choosing farms over sheep does not by itself authorize strategic change.

**FO-05 — Food shortage is evidence, not objective.**

Economic pressure must cross an explicit authority boundary before becoming strategic action.

**FO-06 — Farm requirement is not farm completion.**

A build request is not an operational farm.

**FO-07 — Reservation is not consumption.**

Protected resources remain attributable until actually consumed or explicitly released.

**FO-08 — Emergency does not erase ownership.**

Emergency food policy must not destroy unrelated commitments.

**FO-09 — Source switching requires explicit cause.**

Do not permit unexplained oscillation.

**FO-10 — Static food rules are not runtime food proof.**

Source presence, rule reachability, and successful gathering are separate evidence classes.

# 41. Final engineering directive to Muse

Do not build a “food priority system.” Build a **food continuity subsystem**.

The donor has already accumulated specialized knowledge for sheep, luring, forage, farms, worker allocation, and transitions. The correct engineering action is extraction plus controlled improvement, not wholesale replacement. The forensic baseline explicitly classifies food logistics as **PRESERVE + IMPROVE**. fileciteturn32file0L2-L2

The architecture should become:

```text
OBSERVE FOOD WORLD
        ↓
CLASSIFY SOURCES
        ↓
ESTIMATE SUPPLY / DEMAND
        ↓
DETERMINE CONTINUITY RISK
        ↓
DECLARE SOURCE / WORKER / INFRASTRUCTURE REQUIREMENT
        ↓
EXECUTE THROUGH EXISTING ECONOMIC AUTHORITY
        ↓
OBSERVE ACTUAL RESULT
        ↓
VERIFY
        ↓
REASSESS
```

The critical boundary is:

```text
AEGIS / ECONOMIC AUTHORITY
        ↓
FOOD REQUIREMENT
        ↓
FOOD LOGISTICS
        ↓
VILLAGER / CONSTRUCTION EXECUTION
        ↓
OBSERVED FOOD FLOW
```

Food logistics should become better at answering:

> **Can the current economy sustain the food demand imposed upon it, and what operational intervention is required before continuity fails?**

It should not answer:

> **What strategic plan should the civilization pursue?**

That remains outside System 05.

The engineering objective is therefore:

> **Turn Shadow's procedural food rules into an auditable, forward-looking food continuity service that preserves its specialized source knowledge, distinguishes supply from demand, binds infrastructure and worker requirements to explicit economic authority, detects transition risk before starvation, and reports verified food-state evidence upward without becoming a second strategic planner.**
